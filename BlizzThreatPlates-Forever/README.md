# BlizzThreatPlates-Forever

Make enemy threat easier to read with customizable nameplates, threat colors, and target-of-target names for WoW Forever.

Choose **Blizzard Large**, **Blizzard Default**, **ATD Inspired**, or **Jundies Inspired**. ATD adds a compact bar with the enemy name and health percentage inside, a white selected-target border with a soft glow, and a matching castbar. Each style remembers its size and text settings.

**Nameplates > Style > Liquid Threat Animation** opens a detail page inside the existing options window. Its style chooser switches between the public styles without leaving the page; choose ATD Inspired or Jundies Inspired to enable the effect. The toggle starts disabled and remembers its setting and Liquid Speed across reloads and updates. Liquid Speed defaults to 1.5x; saved speed choices are preserved. Other styles cannot enable it; attempts show a blinking red requirement. Play Animation previews the full progression without applying simulated values to actual mobs. The Threat Loss slider holds a chosen preview mix while Play Animation keeps the liquid moving. Reset disables liquid and restores 1.5x speed. Three sample bars show green/yellow at 30%, yellow warning, and yellow/red at 91%. Orange folds appear in the early yellow range; red begins after 65% visual threat loss. The live effect follows threat with the options closed; disabled live plates allocate no liquid textures, and its animation driver stops when no affected plates are visible.

General omits its extra page heading, divider and outer outline, giving more space to the **What's New** bullet list, ending with a small animated Liquid Threat preview and **Try Me** shortcut. The feedback card has its own padding and bottom margin. Update the highlights alongside release notes when public features are added.

**Nameplates > Combo Points**, or **/btpcombo**, opens a full-size page inside the main options window. A top card holds Enable on Nameplates and Preview on Target side by side. The selected nameplate style appears in a shared Style-sized preview, with sample combo points at the full fitted scale. Sample debuffs are omitted to keep the bar and gems large. Choose Above or Below; Below temporarily moves above during enemy casts/channels, then returns when the cast ends. Size, X Placement, Y Placement, Spacing and Opacity update immediately. X/Y and placement save separately for each style and survive reloads and updates. Defaults are Above, spacing 4px, size 16px, opacity 100%; Y is ATD 6px, Jundies 5px, Blizzard Large 8px and Blizzard Default 2px. Reset restores common defaults and only the current style position. Existing experimental height/placement migrate onto the current style. Top debuffs and target-aura previews reserve space only when needed; restricted resource values hide the live row. Native combo gain animations and Blizzard red remain intact, with dark grey rims. No Blizzard unit-frame or real AuraButton scripts are changed.

Idle neutral liquid now uses 34%. Quest-status caches verify the unit's readable GUID, so switching away from a quest mob cannot lend its classification to the next target for the five-second cache lifetime. Cosmetic mixes update immediately rather than easing from a previous idle classification.

The live combo row uses Blizzard's native gain/rim transitions when readable combo points change, including the first gain after spending all points. Target changes and initial state snapshots load instantly; ordinary aura/layout refreshes preserve any active transition. The static three-point preview does not simulate gaining a point.

**Liquid Swirl** adds optional evolving currents inside filled combo points, mixing deep crimson, scarlet, burnt orange and amber with shaded depth. Moving eddies reshape the pigment instead of rotating a fixed image. The effect starts enabled, preserving existing saved opt-outs, and runs at a fixed 1.60x speed using the red/orange palette. There are no public color or speed controls. Each gem uses independent native flipbook timing and phase, with no addon per-frame loop. One 512x1024 RGBA atlas ships under media/combo. Textures allocate only for enabled filled points; spent, hidden or zero-opacity points stop playback. Leaving the page stops its sample while visible real points continue. Rebuild the atlas and illustrative contact sheet with tools/Build-ComboSwirl.ps1.

With Liquid Threat enabled, the selected out-of-combat quest mob uses the **73%** visual mix, a hostile non-quest mob uses **97%**, and a neutral mob uses **34%**. Other idle mobs retain their normal colors. These mixes do not change threat numbers. They stop when the player or selected mob enters combat; the live renderer resumes without retaining a cosmetic value as real threat. Friendly non-quest NPCs and tapped mobs are excluded, and restricted reaction/combat values do not drive cosmetic classification.

The three-mode threat introduction appears once for both new installs and existing users during the Mixed rollout, preserving their selected mode until they choose another. Closing it or choosing a mode acknowledges it across reloads and later builds; a combat interruption resumes safely. Try Me keeps its gold border, and Style and Enemy Debuffs keep their sidebar NEW markers.

**Jundies Inspired** is another choice under **Nameplates > Style**. It is an independent visual interpretation of the public [Jundies Plater screenshots](https://wago.io/ak3iS95aa): flat rectangular bars, no non-target rim, a one-pixel light selected outline with a one-pixel black inset at 65% opacity, Expressway Bold text, and abbreviated health values beside percentages inside the bar. It defaults to nameplate size **2**, **84% width**, **104% height**, and **12 px** for the enemy name, health percentage and health value, with both health fields enabled. Text shadows are off. The base health bar is 180x20 before those dimensions; the castbar is 116.424x12 at the default width (138.6 before width scaling). Style Reset restores these size/text/dimension defaults, while saved user adjustments remain intact when switching styles. Size/shape, text sizes, health-text visibility and castbar controls remain available; health-text and castbar preferences are separate from ATD. Switching styles retains the previous choices. The style uses BTP's existing threat/quest/player colors and aura/CC handling; it does not import Plater's dungeon-specific colors, scaling rules, mods or scripts. Compatibility Mode uses Blizzard's normal frames. Unlike creator drafts, this built-in style ships in public builds.

For Blizzard styles, **Nameplates > Style > Disable Mob Level** hides the numbered level bubble when checked and updates the preview. It starts unchecked once for fresh installs and updates, then remembers your choice across reloads and future builds. Compatibility Mode retains Blizzard's display.

The options window and threat-choice welcome splash use **#0E1013**, with **10% background transparency** and **opaque header/footer**. These production values preserve the chosen appearance with Dev Only hidden and excluded from release ZIPs. Dev Only is disabled locally; CurseForge packaging always excludes the developer modules and preserves the working copy's setting.

## Choose your threat display

**Threat Percentages** show live Blizzard-supplied percentages on enemies with available threat data, including enemies you are not targeting. Useful for watching several enemies at once. Percentages can exceed 100%; they are not a threat-point difference.

**Threat Differential + Snapshots** shows how many threat points you are ahead (+) or behind (-) compared with the highest group member or pet. Only the selected enemy updates live. When you switch targets, its last known number remains briefly with a `*`, then blinks and fades. Select that enemy again to refresh it.

**Mixed** shows the live threat-point difference on your selected target and live percentages on other enemies. If the target's difference is unavailable, it uses percentage when available. Mixed uses no snapshots.

**Compatibility Mode** starts with threat text only. Under Advanced > Compatibility Mode > Optional Features, opt into target-of-target, quest colors/icons, combo points, recoloring, brightness, aggro flashing or enemy debuffs. These choices survive reloads; switching the mode off restores the normal feature settings. Full style, size and text replacement remain disabled while another addon owns the bars.

**Target of Target** has a separate Show on Friendly Nameplates toggle. **Style > Text Size** has independent Enemy Name, Health Percentage and Health Value toggles and alignment controls (Default, Left, Center, Right), saved per style and reflected in the preview. Reset restores the current style's default alignment.

All three modes require comparison data from you and another group member or pet. Blizzard's restrictions can prevent a value from appearing. All modes support an optional Only When Assigned Tank setting. Your existing mode stays selected when updating; the retired experimental Total Threat mode falls back to Differential.

Under **Colors & Threat > Threat Display**, customize **Have Aggro**, **Warning**, and **Lost Aggro** text colors. Differential and Percentage have separate palettes. In Mixed, use **Target Numbers** and **Percentages** to edit each palette. Opening a color picker previews that state; each Default button restores its original color. Text colors are independent of health bars. The Threat Display menu's NEW marker stays dismissed after opening it; the NEW tag beside Threat Number Colors remains.

## More features

Local development includes **Dev Only > Nameplate Creator**, also opened with **/btpcreator**. Its separate window edits health-bar dimensions, scale, opacity, fill direction and textures; empty-bar backgrounds; normal/selected borders; individual name, health and cast fonts; and castbar geometry/colors. Sample health, target selection, protected casting, name length and zoom update the fitted preview immediately.

Under **Fonts**, choose a **Text Element** to adjust its **Drop Shadow**, horizontal/vertical shadow offsets, opacity and color. The outline is controlled separately. Shadow edits update the sample and live target preview; Save Style or Save Draft preserves them across reloads. Existing shadows keep the previous black color, full opacity and 1/-1 offset until edited.

After **Reset Style**, use **Save Style** to commit the reset. Main Style width/height controls read the saved design's geometry, and saving an active built-in also synchronizes its ordinary percentage settings. The next width/height adjustment starts from that saved geometry instead of an old percentage.

For **Jundies Inspired > Borders**, **Non-target Thickness/Color/Opacity** affect only unselected plates. **Target Thickness/Color/Opacity** control the selected outer rim. The Jundies-only **Selected Black Inset Thickness/Opacity** controls adjust its inner black edge independently; existing designs keep the previous 1px/65% inset. Toggle **Selected Target** in the editor to compare the two states before saving.

The **Style Library** lists the installed Blizzard styles, **ATD Inspired**, **Jundies Inspired**, and saved drafts. Opening the creator selects the style currently used in game. Selecting a built-in also activates it outside combat. ATD and Jundies support the full editor: unsaved edits preview on the target; **Save Style** updates that same built-in locally on all nameplates, including after closing the editor, in combat and across reloads. Their saved designs remain independent. Reset Section/Style returns to the appearance captured before the first edit; save to keep the reset. Unchanged ATD borders retain the original selected outline and glow. **Duplicate** creates a separate editable draft, and built-ins cannot be renamed or deleted. The older Jundies starting draft is preserved independently.

Blizzard styles retain their native artwork and expose their supported size, width/height, enemy-name size, health percentage/value sizes, and health-text visibility controls. These settings apply and save immediately through the existing native options; combat disables them. The game nameplate is the native live preview. Native custom fonts, textures, border thickness and arbitrary text/castbar layout are not exposed in the creator; choose ATD or Jundies for those controls.

The **Health Bar > Auto-fit Name & Health** option keeps health numbers together at the right and gives the name the remaining space. Turn it off to use each text element's individual anchor and width controls. The built-in overrides are local developer settings, excluded with the creator modules from public ZIPs; they do not automatically change the shipped style definitions.

**Dev Only > Window > Liquid Threat Lab**, or **/btplava**, opens the local **Acrylic Pour** experiment. Acrylic is the only material; selections of retired variants migrate to Acrylic. The lab shares the public renderer's four precomputed 128-frame atlases and six reused texture regions. Generation is offline. The lab driver runs only while opened or following live threat, and closing an idle preview stops it.

Acrylic starts with the former 10% green/yellow mix, rejoins the existing blend at 20%, preserves the 30% balance, clears green through a shaded yellow bridge at 36%, and introduces localized orange folds on yellow around 39%. Red begins blending into those folds after 65%, reaching the original red palette by 81%. Pale-yellow highlights and deeper gold/amber marbling give the yellow range more depth. The final quarter maps to the former 75-81% range, keeping the chosen animated red/yellow mix at 100%. Local currents change direction independently. Live lead calibration uses 160% or higher for the 0% mix, a 100% tie/zero raw differential for 35%, 94% lead for 38%, and 50% or lower for the chosen 100% mix.

Use Threat Loss, Play/Pause Cycle and Liquid Speed to adjust the preview. Nameplate Test offers **Preview only**, **Simulate on target**, and **Follow live threat** for Jundies, ATD and saved drafts. Live mode uses readable lead or a complete readable player/group/pet raw comparison. If values become restricted after unselecting a mob, its last readable mix is retained while the readable threat state stays unchanged. Fresh readable data updates it; a state change, combat ending, plate removal/reuse or stopping live mode clears it. Without a matching retained mix, restricted/incomplete data uses native-state fallback. Quest/tapped/player colors are preserved; secure solo attacks use the 0% mix until another player or pet has threat. The status line shows the target's applied progression and source.

Follow live threat continues with the lab closed and across zone changes. Reopen it and choose Preview only, or use Reset, to restore normal colors. Closing a target simulation or hiding Dev Only also restores normal colors. The hidden preview does not repaint. Live mode resets after reload, and saved palettes are unchanged. The unused materials and older textures are archived under local backups. Offline tools are `tools/New-LiquidThreatVariants.ps1` and `tools/Preview-LiquidThreat.ps1`; their former `-AcrylicOnly` switch remains accepted.

CurseForge packaging excludes all developer modules and TOC entries, every `devmedia` texture, and the inline lab callback from `ATDStyle.lua`. The public addon includes the separate saved Liquid Threat feature and its four assets under `media/liquid`, while the lab slash command, Dev Only menu, Nameplate Creator and draft choices are excluded. Packaging verifies both the public files and the absence of developer tools. Local Dev Only tools remain enabled after packaging.

Texture buttons open a searchable, scrollable browser with preview swatches. Eleven built-in choices include Flat, two Blizzard textures, and eight original BTP textures: Soft, Bevel, Gloss, Matte, Brushed, Diagonal, Segments and Satin. Optional LibSharedMedia statusbar registrations appear automatically when the browser opens; Refresh Media discovers later registrations. Custom File / ID accepts an installed `Interface\...` texture path or a positive file ID in the editor's Texture File / ID field (press Enter). Newly installed files may require restarting the game. Drafts and exports retain resolved custom/shared texture paths, but exporting does not copy or bundle external texture files.

The font browser includes eighteen built-in choices, including Expressway Regular/Bold, Roboto Condensed Bold, Anton, Bebas Neue, Rajdhani regular/bold, IBM Plex Mono regular/bold, IBM Plex Sans Condensed regular/bold, and Cinzel Decorative. It displays sample lettering, discovers optional SharedMedia fonts, and supports installed `Interface\...` or `Fonts\...` `.ttf`/`.otf` paths. Each text element keeps its own font. Resolved font paths survive draft saves and exports. Font sources and licenses are in `media/fonts`.

Creator working changes survive reloads. Save Draft stores a library snapshot and adds **Draft: <name>** to **Nameplates > Style** in local development. Choose it outside combat to use the saved design on all nameplates, including in combat and after closing the creator or reloading. Opening the creator automatically previews unsaved edits on your visible target when using ATD, Jundies or a draft style outside combat. Slider, font, texture and color edits update the live target without saving; preview follows target changes and resumes when a visible target becomes available or combat ends. Pause Preview keeps it paused until Resume Preview or the next editor opening. Closing the creator or hiding Dev Only restores the saved appearance; other plates keep their saved style throughout. Save Draft keeps the design and updates the active snapshot. The normal Style page keeps its Size & Shape, Text Size and Reset controls for built-ins and saved drafts; the Castbar controls also remain available. These controls update saved designs without replacing their other customizations. The Nameplate Creator stays under Dev Only, with no shortcut card replacing regular settings. Switching to Blizzard or ATD restores that style; deleting an active draft outside combat switches back to ATD. New/Duplicate and switching drafts preserve current work; Undo/Redo and section/style resets support iteration. Copy Style exports validated Lua style data for later packaging and does not publish a design. Icon and aura settings retain the existing ATD layout. Creator modules and draft style choices are excluded from CurseForge ZIPs; a previously active draft falls back to ATD there.

## Installation and updating

Extract the `BlizzThreatPlates-Forever` folder into your WoW `Interface/AddOns` folder and enable the addon. Keep your SavedVariables when updating to preserve your settings.

Open settings with the minimap button. Select your threat mode under **Colors & Threat** and your appearance under **Nameplates**.

General appears as the greeting page each time you open the UI. Selecting another group hides General until the next opening. The active group moves to the top of the sidebar with its submenus beneath it; the remaining groups follow below.

On first selection, ATD uses nameplate size **4**, enemy name **11 px**, health percentage **11 px**, and health value **12 px**. Existing saved ATD customizations are preserved.

Version **1.4.4**. Designed for WoW Forever; ATD is an included appearance, not a Plater profile importer. Plater is not required.

This release includes a **one-time sizing reset** that adopts the style currently selected in Blizzard's options and resets that style to size **1**, width **100%**, and height **100%**. This runs once for new and existing installs, including saved ATD appearances; ATD can be selected again afterward. Users who already received this reset in a recent test build are not reset again. Later size/shape/style choices stay saved across reloads, and other styles' stored dimensions and existing text/color preferences are retained. Protected size changes wait until combat ends.

Fresh native installs retain Blizzard's selected style and use size **1**, name **11 px**, health percentage **12 px**, percentage-only health information, and **100%** native aura scale. On first loading this release, both new and existing installs have friendly **Open World > Use Class Color** and **Use Class Colors for Enemy Players** enabled once. Later choices stay saved across reloads and future upgrades. Friendly class colors start disabled in Battlegrounds, Dungeons, and Raids on new installs; updates preserve existing choices for those locations. Reset/Default buttons use the new sizing baseline. Health Value size is disabled while numeric health is not displayed.

Enable Compatibility Mode under **Advanced** in the modern layout to use threat numbers and optional target-of-target text alongside another nameplate addon. Threat Display and Nameplates > Target of Target offer independent placement controls with previews. Large percentages display compactly, for example 6.3K%.

Large Style previews scale down uniformly to fit their box; the preview label shows the displayed scale when reduced. Blizzard Default centers its sample name above the bar.

Width and Height range from **50% to 150%** of the selected size, defaulting to **100%**, and are saved separately for Blizzard Large, Blizzard Default, and ATD. Change shape outside combat; Reset restores the current style's default size, fonts, and proportions.

Under **Style > Text Size**, the three sliders are stacked on the left. **Health Percentage** and **Health Value** toggles sit beside their size rows on the right, and the preview follows both choices. Health Value starts disabled at **12 px** on new installs, ready to use at that size when enabled; saved sizes and visibility choices are preserved. Hidden health fields have their size sliders dimmed. Blizzard styles mirror the corresponding Nameplate Information options in Blizzard's menu, preserving other information flags and queuing protected changes until combat ends. ATD has independent saved toggles and its own numeric-health label, with percentage enabled and numeric value disabled initially. Switching styles keeps the two sets of visibility choices separate. Reset changes sizes and proportions while retaining visibility choices.

While BTP's Target of Target is enabled, Blizzard's Spell Target castbar label is turned off and its BTP menu option is grayed out. Turning Target of Target off restores the previous Spell Target preference. Changes made during combat apply afterward.

ATD castbar sliders update the preview while dragging. Castbars stay aligned with the health bar's visible left edge, including the white outline when selected, and follow nameplate Size and Width adjustments. Increasing castbar width extends it to the right. Their default baseline width of 130 matches the health bar; custom widths are retained, and Castbar Reset restores the matching width.

Enemy Debuffs follows the style selected in Style for both its settings and preview, without a separate style selector. The PvE/PvP buttons sit above the preview, aligned to its right edge. PvE controls NPC settings; PvP controls enemy-player settings. Blizzard styles expose the native Buffs/Debuffs dropdown, Buff/Debuff Scale, and Debuff Padding. ATD shows its own visibility, size, spacing, and count controls. Preview sliders update immediately; native setting changes during combat apply afterward. Native scale and padding are shared game settings across both contexts.

The modern sidebar has four main groups: **General**, **Colors & Threat**, **Nameplates**, and **Advanced**. Every opening through the minimap button or /btp starts at General, which disappears from navigation when you leave it. The active group moves to the top, with other groups below a thin separator. Clicking Colors & Threat selects Threat Colors and keeps its submenu expanded, including when that group is already active. Clicking Nameplates opens Style. Main groups retain thin borders, gold hover trim, and their existing highlights; only the selected submenu uses gold text. Menu NEW markers start unread for fresh installs and users updating to this feature. Opening a group or tagged submenu permanently dismisses its own marker across reloads and future builds; unvisited siblings keep theirs. Opening the default subsection counts as visiting it. NEW tags beside individual options remain until removed from the addon. Style, Enemy Debuffs, and Quest Icons share the same NEW position. The background, header, and footer use the flat production colors described above; navigation and content panels remain opaque.

Experimental **Colors & Threat > Border Colors (Still Testing)** has a permanent submenu NEW marker and a red testing notice. Selected Target / Non-Target tabs share one settings card and a matching preview. Choose Original, Custom Color, or Threat Color (Use Threat Colors on Non-Target); only relevant controls appear. Both modes start on Original once for fresh installs and updates, including prior experiment settings; later choices persist across reloads and future builds. Reset Both Borders restores original appearances. Dev Only is hidden and excluded from public packages.

Threat Colors starts with the selected-style preview and **Default Colors**, **Tank Colors**, and **DPS / Healer Colors** palette tabs. Default Quest Color is the fourth row of the Default card. One compact card below holds **Disable Quest Color** on the left and **Auto-Switch Role Colors** with NEW on the right. Its label is white while disabled and gold while enabled. A right-aligned Current line just below the compact card shows the active palette and location, such as Current: Default (Open World). Hover over the role checkbox for its assigned-role explanation and full active-palette/fallback status: switching is off, you are outside a dungeon/raid, or no group role is assigned. Auto-Switch Role Colors preserves the previous Dungeon & Raid Role Colors on/off choice when updating. Fresh installs start with Auto-Switch off. Later choices remain saved across reloads and future updates. Existing saved colors and dungeon/raid assigned-role behavior are preserved. Tank Colors and DPS / Healer Colors can be opened and edited when Auto-Switch Role Colors is enabled, including while in the open world. Clicking either tab while it is off keeps Default Colors visible and briefly pulses a gold outline around the required checkbox. Turning Auto-Switch off returns to Default Colors without deleting saved role colors. Choosing a palette does not change your assigned role; the preview names the palette being edited, and its upward-opening selector previews threat states or quest coloring. Opening a color wheel selects its matching preview; edits, Cancel, and Default preserve the correct palette across tab changes. Quest coloring stays independent; disabling it shows the fallback and a notice in its preview. The role palettes retain the Off-Tank testing indicator.

**Nameplates > Quest Icons** contains Quest Objective Icons, a selected-style preview, and icon size/position sliders on one page. Existing visibility, size, and position choices carry over. The sample stays visible while icons are disabled so it can still be configured; its caption identifies that state. Reset restores size and position while preserving visibility and quest coloring. Quest icons can remain visible without recoloring the health bar. The separate modern Customize Icons window has been removed.

Threat Display keeps its mode selector, selected-style preview, and visibility toggles on the main page. Its **Customize** button opens a matching window with the same preview and the existing text-size, position, and snapshot-duration sliders. A compact Percentage/Differential/Mixed selector sits above the editor preview, with Reset immediately to its left. The two mode selectors stay synchronized. Changes update both previews immediately and remain saved when closing or reopening the editor. Snapshot Duration applies only to Threat Differential. Reset restores size/position while retaining mode, duration, and visibility choices. The selectors use white text with their gold highlight.

Nameplates > Target of Target uses the same Blizzard Large/Default/ATD sample renderer as Style. Its framed Name Size and X/Y sliders update the preview immediately, with clickable font-size notches, Enable Target of Target and Show Last Name toggles, and Reset for size/position only. The complete plate and target label fit the preview even at extreme offsets. Target text renders above the health/castbar artwork on live plates and previews, including when its position overlaps a Blizzard bar. Saved target settings and the native Spell Target interlock are retained.

Target of Target defaults to **13 px, X 3, Y 0** for ATD and **12 px, X -5, Y 2** for Blizzard Large/Default. ATD and Blizzard retain separate size/position choices; Reset restores the active style's defaults. Existing shared size/position overrides migrate to the style they were used with. Show Last Name starts disabled on fresh installs, and later choices remain saved.

Player Colors groups enemy PvP color/class-color controls and friendly player colors. The Friendly Players card is marked Experimental - Needs Testing in plain gold text. Friendly players have independent Open World (including towns), Battlegrounds, Dungeons, and Raids rows, each with **Use Class Color**, a color swatch, and a compact **Default** button. Click a swatch to enable its custom color; Default restores native coloring while retaining the last custom value privately. Every row displays a solid color: Default shows its native location color, while Custom shows your chosen color. Reset/cancel updates the box immediately, and the picker opens on the displayed color. The Battlegrounds preference also covers arenas if supported by the client. These are location preferences, not separate party/raid membership overrides.

Default swatches start from the Forever client's native palette: purple for ordinary friendly players, green for battleground party members, and lavender for party members in dungeons/raids. Readable live native relationship colors are sampled and remembered per location, independently of any BTP custom paint. Individual native colors can vary with PvP flags and group membership; the default swatch represents a sampled ally rather than forcing all allies to one color. NPCs, dead or disconnected players, and restricted values cannot overwrite the sample.

Each location's Use Class Color choice works with both native styles and ATD. It grays only that row's swatch/Default controls without erasing its saved color. The addon applies the active location's choice to Blizzard's Friendly Players class-color setting when you enter the world or change locations.
